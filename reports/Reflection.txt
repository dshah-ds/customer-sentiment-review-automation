Reflection — GenAI Case Study: Customer Sentiment & Review Response Automation

This project walked through building a GenAI-powered system to help a customer support team
understand and respond to product reviews faster, without losing accuracy or consistency.

Milestone 1 showed that star ratings alone are not a reliable signal — nearly half of all reviews
(45%) showed a mismatch between their star rating and their actual text sentiment, and 78% of
"neutral" 3-star reviews were actually positive in tone. Sentiment analysis (VADER) and keyword-based
theme extraction were needed to separate genuine emotional venting from concrete, actionable product
issues like "defective," "broken," or "refund."

Milestone 2 built on this by segmenting reviews into Positive, Neutral, and Negative groups and
measuring each one numerically (sentiment score, urgency score, review length, and TF-IDF vocabulary).
This confirmed the three segments are genuinely distinct customer populations that need different
treatment — Negative reviews demand fast escalation, Positive reviews are an advocacy opportunity, and
Neutral reviews are an easily overlooked but real opportunity to convert lukewarm satisfaction into
loyalty. This step also surfaced how AI-driven segmentation can carry its own biases (e.g., a keyword
list built from this dataset may not generalize), which is worth auditing regularly rather than trusting
blindly.

Milestone 3 turned these insights into a working tool: a packaged NLP/sentiment pipeline wired into a
Gradio app that predicts sentiment and urgency, extracts themes, and drafts a GenAI-generated response
for a support agent to review. Testing the app across real examples surfaced a genuine limitation — a
generic keyword ("money") caused a false-positive "Refund Request" theme on a review that was simply
commenting on value for price, not asking for a refund. This was a valuable, honest reminder of why the
system is designed as a Human-in-the-Loop assistant rather than a fully automated responder: AI output
should support an agent's judgment, not replace it.

Overall, the project demonstrated how GenAI can automate sentiment analysis and response generation
using real product reviews stored in a database. Ensuring safe, policy-aligned responses requires
careful prompt engineering, transparent handling of known limitations, and consistent human oversight
at the final step. The system significantly improves customer support efficiency by highlighting urgent
reviews and generating consistent, reviewable draft replies — while making clear that responsible GenAI
use means keeping a person in control of what actually reaches the customer.

One additional note for future iterations: this dataset showed signs of being templated/synthetically
generated (narrow review length distribution, repeated phrasing patterns). The techniques built here —
sentiment scoring, segmentation, theme extraction, and the Gradio assistant — worked well and are ready
to apply, but findings and keyword lists should be re-validated against organic, real-world review data
before being relied on in a live production environment.
